# Bidur Ji - Basis of Harsh Perception: An Evidence-Based Analysis of Indian Highway Standards vs Global Benchmarks

**Date:** November 14, 2025
**Research Period:** 2020-2025
**Sources:** Government Reports, Academic Studies, International Assessments

---

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive research evaluates the controversial claim that only 3-4 Indian highways meet global standards. Based on extensive analysis of government data, academic research, and international comparisons, **the claim is factually inaccurate but reflects a deeper reality: only approximately 4-6% of India's total highway network truly meets international controlled-access highway standards.**

---

## 1.0 Introduction and Research Context

### 1.1 Research Objective
To evaluate whether Indian highways, particularly National Highways and Expressways, meet global standards in terms of design, access control, traffic flow, and safety, and to assess the validity of claims regarding the limited number of "true highways" in India.

### 1.2 Methodology
This analysis draws on:
- Government of India data (NHAI, Ministry of Road Transport & Highways)
- Academic research from IIT Delhi, University of Reading, and other institutions
- International comparative studies
- World Economic Forum assessments
- Industry reports and expert analyses

## 5.0 Infrastructure Deficiencies and Challenges

### 5.1 Access Control Limitations

**Key Issues:**
- **At-grade intersections** on many "National Highways"
- **Direct property access** from highways in rural/urban areas
- **Inconsistent median opening controls**
- **Missing or inadequate service roads**

**NHAI Assessment:** Only expressways achieve full access control; most 4-6 lane highways have partial access control.

### 5.2 Construction Quality Challenges

**Material and Design Issues:**
- **Climate mismatch:** Roads designed for 25mm rainfall face 200-400% of design capacity
- **Water-permeable materials** causing premature deterioration
- **"Designed to fail" characteristics** with 2-3 year service life vs 10-15 year design life

**Quality Control Systems:**
- **Pavement Condition Index (PCI)** now mandatory for contractor ratings
- **80% weightage** to PCI in contractor performance evaluation
- **Network Survey Vehicles** for objective quality measurement

### 5.3 Maintenance and Operational Challenges

**Systemic Issues:**
- **Fragmented maintenance responsibilities** across multiple agencies
- **Insufficient maintenance funding** relative to network size
- **Inconsistent quality standards** across different highway classes
- **Enforcement challenges** for speed and access control

## 8.0 The "3-4 True Highways" Claim: Evidence-Based Evaluation

### 8.1 Claim Assessment

**The Claim:** "Only 3-4 roads in India qualify as true highways by global benchmarks"

**Evidence-Based Evaluation:**

**✅ Factually Incorrect:**
- At least **15-20 expressways** meet global standards for controlled access
- **6,059 km** of operational expressways exist, not just 3-4 roads

**⚠️ Reflects Deeper Reality:**
- Only **4-6%** of total highway network meets international standards
- **Vast majority** (94-96%) below global controlled-access benchmarks
- **Quality gaps** significant even on nominally upgraded highways

### 8.2 Reality-Based Assessment

**Highways Truly Meeting Global Standards: ~15-20 corridors**
**Highways with Partial Compliance: ~8,000-12,000 km**
**Highways Not Meeting Standards: ~120,000+ km**

## 9.0 Conclusions and Recommendations

### 9.1 Research Conclusions

1. **The "3-4 highways" claim is an oversimplification** but reflects genuine concerns about standards compliance
2. **India has made remarkable progress** in highway construction (2.8x increase in construction speed)
3. **Quality gaps remain significant** despite quantity achievements
4. **Access control represents the biggest divergence** from global standards
5. **Safety performance falls far below international benchmarks**

### 9.2 Key Challenges

**Immediate Challenges:**
- Climate-adapted design specifications
- Consistent access control implementation
- Quality assurance and maintenance systems
- Safety infrastructure improvements

**Long-term Challenges:**
- Achieving 50,000 km access-controlled network by 2047
- Matching international safety performance
- Sustainable funding mechanisms
- Technology adoption and automation

### 9.3 Recommendations

**Technical Improvements:**
- Update design standards for climate resilience
- Mandate full access control for new "expressway" projects
- Implement scientific quality measurement across entire network
- Standardize safety infrastructure requirements

**Institutional Reforms:**
- Strengthen NHAI rating system with public disclosure
- Develop unified maintenance responsibility framework
- Enhance contractor accountability mechanisms
- Invest in advanced construction technology

**Strategic Priorities:**
- Focus on quality over quantity in future expansion
- Prioritize safety infrastructure investment
- Develop comprehensive access-control implementation strategy
- Establish international benchmarking partnerships
